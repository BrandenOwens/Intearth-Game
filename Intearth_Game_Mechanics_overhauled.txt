0. MODULE ARCHITECTURE (NEW)

Intearth City Duel is transitioning from a single giant Python file to a modular codebase.
This improves maintainability, clarity, and allows AI (ChatGPT) to safely update code one module at a time without breaking the whole project.

0.1 Why Use Modules?

Prevents file-size problems

Prevents merge confusion

Allows the AI to operate on isolated systems

Lets the main file act as an orchestrator only

Cleaner debugging

Code can be replaced in small chunks instead of rewriting 8k–12k lines

0.3 How Modules Work Together

Each module handles one subsystem:

Module	Role
projectiles.py	All projectile logic (wand, bow, staff, splash, update loop)
fighters.py	Player + Enemy movement, combat, hitboxes, knockback
weapons.py	Weapon class, stat generation, rarity
items.py	Shared base item class + armor
citygen.py	Stage generation, platforms, background, merchants, enemies
ui_shop.py	Merchant shop UI
ui_pause.py	Pause / inventory UI
helpers.py	Utility functions (merchant proximity detection, etc.)
constants.py	Global values (WORLD_WIDTH, GROUND_Y, colors, gravity)
main.py	The only file that runs the game & ties modules together
0.4 How AI Should Interact

When you want a change:

You specify the module (ex: “update fighters.py”)

You upload that module from your PC

AI updates the full module and returns a complete replacement

You save the updated file back into the folder

This avoids partial edits and ensures the game stays consistent.

Intearth City Duel - Game Mechanics Document
===========================================

Version: 0.3
Last updated: 2025-11-19

OVERVIEW
--------
Intearth City Duel is a 2D platformer arena set on the rooftops and upper
levels of a stylized city. The player fights enemies, climbs between platforms,
and trades with merchants that appear in each Area.

Each "Area" (or stage) is a slice of city with:
- Multiple vertical platforms (building-like ledges)
- Ladders and doors for vertical travel
- Enemies with stage-scaled stats and some visual variety
- Exactly one merchant that can buy and sell equipment

The design goal is to have combat driven by clearly readable weapons,
projectiles, and knockback, with enemies that feel like individuals rather than
identical clones.

CORE LOOP
---------
- Spawn into an Area with multiple platforms and a skyline background.
- Move, jump, and climb to traverse the level.
- Fight enemies using melee or ranged weapons.
- Collect money and gear dropped from enemies.
- Find the merchant and trade for better equipment.
- Reach the far right edge of the level to advance to the next Area.

STAGE & PLATFORM DESIGN
-----------------------
- WORLD_WIDTH defines the horizontal size of each city slice.
- The ground is a long, solid platform at y = GROUND_Y.
- Additional platforms are generated at different heights, giving a
  multi-level "building rooftop" feel.
- Many platforms have ladders that connect them down toward the ground.
- Doors are placed near platforms (visual only for now) to suggest building
  entrances.

Vertical Travel
- Player can jump between platforms.
- Ladders allow controlled climbing up and down using W/S.
- While climbing, gravity is suppressed so actors do not slide off.

Background Buildings
- Two parallax-like layers of buildings are generated per Area:
  - Back layer: tall silhouettes, darker and further away.
  - Front layer: lower buildings, slightly brighter.
- These use random widths and heights to create a varied skyline.

MERCHANTS
---------
Design:
- Exactly one merchant per Area, placed on a reachable non-ground platform.

Current behavior (code target):
- During Area generation, one platform is chosen as the merchant platform.
- A Fighter with is_merchant=True is spawned there.

Merchant rules:
- Merchants use the Fighter class but are flagged is_merchant = True.
- Merchants do not move or attack.
- Merchants have their own inventory and money pool.
- Player can open the shop UI by pressing E when standing near a merchant.
- Merchants can buy items from the player and sell items to the player.
- Merchants do not currently drop loot when killed (if killing is allowed).

PLAYER STATS
------------
- HP: starts at 100.
- Damage: base damage plus weapon damage.
- Armor: reduces incoming damage on hit.
- Attack delay (in frames): lower delay = faster attacks.
- Money: used to buy items from merchants.

HP Regeneration
- Only the player regenerates HP over time.
- After taking damage, there is a 3-second delay (regen_cooldown) before
  regen can start.
- Once regen starts, the player regains 1 HP per second until full.

ITEMS & INVENTORY
-----------------
Two main item categories:

- Weapons
  - Fields: dmg, atk_speed_bonus, knockback, visual, ranged flag
  - May also implicitly define projectile behavior (for ranged types).

- Armor
  - Fields: armor (primary), optionally other modifiers in the future.
  - Armor pieces are thematically split into:
    - Helmet (head slot)
    - Chest armor (torso slot)
    - Leggings (legs slot)
  - For now, armor is mechanically one combined value, but visuals are
    allowed to show separate pieces.

Rarity colors are computed from a power score based on an item's stats:
- Uncommon:    Green
- Rare:        Blue
- Epic:        Purple
- Legendary:   Orange
- Mythic:      Red
- Divine:      Gold (highest scores)

A "common" / gray tier may be implied when stats are weak and no special
rarity color is used.

MELEE WEAPONS (CURRENT TYPES)
-----------------------------
Visual styles (intent):

- Short Sword
  - Straight line blade.
  - Visually compact, least knockback.
- Long Sword
  - T-shaped blade: long blade with cross-guard.
  - Medium knockback.
- Battle Axe
  - Handle with a D-shaped head on the end.
  - Largest knockback of the melee set.

Implementation:
- Melee weapons are generated based on per-stage damage and knockback ranges.
- Enemies and merchants primarily use these melee weapons at present.
- Knockback contribution from melee is determined both by the weapon's
  base knockback stat and by a type-based multiplier (see Knockback System).

RANGED WEAPON TYPES (PLAYER-AVAILABLE)
--------------------------------------
All ranged weapons:
- Are identified by weapon.ranged == True.
- Use projectiles for damage, not melee hitboxes.
- Still play an attack animation, but do not create melee swing hitboxes.

Types:

1) Wand
   Identity:
   - Fast, short-range magic.

   Projectile visual:
   - Small spark "*" drawn as a tiny glowing orb.

   Behavior:
   - High projectile speed.
   - Short projectile range.
   - Slightly lower damage.
   - Faster attack rate.
   - No guaranteed knockback; instead the wand has a low chance to apply a
     small knockback when it rolls a random knockback stat (with a strong
     reduction multiplier so knockback is subtle).

2) Bow
   Identity:
   - Balanced long-range weapon.

   Bow visual (held):
   - True D-shape:
     - String: vertical line anchored near the hand.
     - Limbs: curved / angled forward toward the facing direction, forming an
       open D shape that does not clip at the edge.

   Projectile visual:
   - Arrow "-->" represented as a short horizontal projectile with a
     directional line segment.

   Behavior:
   - Medium projectile speed.
   - Long projectile range.
   - Standard damage and attack rate.
   - Medium knockback on hit.

3) Staff
   Identity:
   - Slow, heavy magic with area-effect impact.

   Staff visual (held):
   - Vertical casting focus:
     - Shaft mostly vertical, anchored to the hand.
     - Slight tilt left/right based on horizontal movement speed.
     - Extra tilt or animation during attacks.

   Projectile visual:
   - Central orb with spikes in all directions, conceptually:

         *
       * O *
         *

   Behavior:
   - Slow projectile speed.
   - Medium-long projectile range.
   - High damage.
   - Strong knockback.
   - Splash damage around the hit location.

KNOCKBACK SYSTEM (MELEE + RANGED)
---------------------------------
Knockback is used to make combat feel less "sticky" and to prevent enemies
from standing directly on top of the player.

Each weapon has a base knockback stat and also receives a type-based modifier.
Higher knockback pushes targets farther away horizontally.

Relative knockback by weapon type:

- Short Sword
  - Lowest melee knockback.
  - Intended as a precise, controlled weapon that does not throw enemies far.

- Long Sword
  - Medium melee knockback.
  - Pushes enemies back a noticeable but manageable distance.

- Battle Axe
  - Highest melee knockback.
  - Strong shove on hit; enemies visibly recoil.

- Wand
  - Base design: no constant knockback.
  - When knockback is rolled for a wand, it is applied at a reduced
    multiplier (subtle push) and may be treated as a low probability effect.

- Bow
  - Medium knockback per hit.
  - Helps keep enemies at range without launching them off-screen.

- Staff
  - Big knockback on direct hits.
  - Splash damage may apply reduced knockback to nearby enemies to avoid
    chaotic scattering.

Projectiles inherit knockback from their weapon and type multipliers.

PROJECTILE SYSTEM (CURRENT DESIGN)
----------------------------------
- A global PROJECTILES list holds all active projectiles.
- When a Fighter with a ranged weapon starts an attack, the code:
  - Flags the attack type as "shoot".
  - Spawns a Projectile instance via a helper function (e.g. spawn_projectile).
- Ranged weapons do NOT create melee hitboxes: get_attack_hitbox() returns
  None for ranged weapons.

Projectile properties:
- Position (x, y)
- Velocity (vx, vy)
- Max travel distance
- Total distance traveled
- Damage and knockback
- Owner (the Fighter that fired it)
- Kind: "wand", "bow", or "staff"
- Color: derived from the weapon's rarity color when available
- Optional splash radius (used for staff projectiles)

Projectile update rules:
- Each frame, projectiles move along a straight line (no gravity, no arc).
- Distance traveled is accumulated; once it exceeds max_distance, the
  projectile is removed.
- Projectiles check for collision with Fighters:
  - The owner is ignored to prevent self hits.
  - On first successful hit:
    - The target takes damage.
    - Knockback is applied based on projectile direction and knockback value.
    - For staff projectiles, nearby enemies in a radius take splash damage.
    - The projectile is then removed.

Projectile visuals:
- Wand: small glowing circle.
- Bow: short horizontal line suggesting an arrow in flight.
- Staff: orb with multiple short spikes drawn outward from the center.

PROCEDURAL WEAPON GENERATION (DESIGN)
-------------------------------------
Base stats to roll per weapon:
- damage: integer in a base range (per stage).
- attack_speed_factor: float that adjusts the attack delay.
- knockback: float in a range (per stage).

For ranged weapons only:
- projectile_speed: float (e.g., 7–14).
- projectile_range: integer (e.g., 250–600).
- splash_radius: for staff-type weapons only.

Type multipliers (example intent):
- Wand:
  - projectile_speed *= 1.5
  - projectile_range *= 0.6
  - damage            *= 0.8
  - attack_speed_factor biased faster
  - knockback reduced or frequently zeroed.

- Bow:
  - projectile_speed *= 1.0
  - projectile_range *= 1.2
  - damage            *= 1.0
  - attack_speed_factor unchanged
  - medium knockback.

- Staff:
  - projectile_speed *= 0.5
  - projectile_range *= 0.8
  - damage            *= 1.6
  - splash_radius set to a positive value
  - high knockback.

Current implementation state:
- Enemy and merchant melee weapons are generated with stage-based ranges
  for damage and knockback.
- Ranged weapons for enemies/merchants and full projectile stat multipliers
  are still a TODO.

Item Pricing:
- Price is computed from damage, knockback, and attack speed bonus, then
  converted into a gold cost.
- Rarity is derived from the combined stat power and can be used to visually
  color the item name or its projectile.

ENEMY BEHAVIOR
--------------
Enemies share the Fighter class (is_player=False, is_merchant=False).

Current simple behaviors:
- If the player is outside an aggro range, enemies stop moving.
- Within aggro range, enemies move horizontally toward the player.
- If the player is above them and conditions are met, enemies may jump.
- Enemies currently use melee attacks only; they call start_attack("swing")
  when close enough.

Design improvements (target behavior):
- Enemies should not stand *directly* on the player while attacking.
- When enemies hit or are hit, knockback should push them backward, creating
  clear spacing and reducing "face-hugging" behavior.
- After being knocked back, enemies:
  - Briefly pause or "recover" before advancing again.
  - Re-acquire the player and close distance using the same aggro logic.

Future behaviors (not yet implemented):
- Ranged enemy variants using bows/wands/staffs.
- Patrol / idle movement when not aggroed.
- Smarter jump decisions for vertical chase.

ENEMY VISUAL VARIETY & EQUIPMENT
--------------------------------
To avoid all enemies looking identical, fighters can be visually varied via:

Body proportions (design):
- Each enemy can receive random scale factors for:
  - Head size (width/height).
  - Torso height.
  - Leg length.
- These scale factors are visual only and do not alter hitbox size in the
  initial implementation (hitbox remains tied to the Fighter rect).

Armor visuals:
- Enemies can spawn with visible armor pieces:
  - Helmet
  - Chest armor
  - Leggings
- These may be mapped to the same underlying armor stat or later split into
  separate armor slots.
- Armor presence can be hooked to the armor value rolled for that enemy:
  - Higher armor → more pieces visible.

Equipment drops:
- Non-merchant enemies have a chance to drop:
  - Their weapon.
  - Their armor (helmet / chest / leggings treated as one armor item for now).
- Drop chance can be tuned per type or globally (e.g., 20–30% chance to drop
  one of their items).
- Dropped gear is instantiated as inventory items when the enemy dies and can
  be added to the player's inventory.

LOOT RULES
----------
Current / target behavior:
- When non-merchant enemies die:
  - Their money is transferred to the player.
  - Optionally, one or more of their equipment items may be awarded to the
    player based on a drop chance.
- Merchants do not drop loot.

SHOP UI
-------
- Player can open the shop UI by pressing E near a merchant.
- Two-column layout:
  - Left: Merchant inventory.
  - Right: Player inventory.
- TAB switches focus between columns.
- UP/DOWN moves the selected item within the active column.
- B buys the selected item (when focus is on merchant column).
- S sells the selected item (when focus is on player column).
- Player and merchant money are tracked separately.

PAUSE MENU / INVENTORY
----------------------
- Press ESC when not trading to open the pause menu.
- The pause menu shows all items in the player's inventory and a "Quit Game"
  entry at the bottom.
- Controls:
  - UP/DOWN to change selection.
  - ENTER to equip/use the selected item or to confirm quit.
  - ESC to close the menu.
- Equipping rules:
  - Items with dmg > 0 equip as weapon.
  - Items with armor > 0 equip as armor.
  - Equipped items are suffix-labeled with [Equipped].

HUD
---
- Top-left HUD displays:
  - HP / Max HP
  - Total Damage
  - Total Armor
  - Current attack delay (frames)
  - Current money
  - Current Area index
- When the player is near a merchant and not in a menu, a hint appears:
  "Press E to trade".
- When the player is dead, a simple death message is shown with instructions
  for exiting or restarting.

AREA PROGRESSION
----------------
- When the player reaches the far right side of WORLD_WIDTH, the game
  increments the Area index.
- A new City instance is generated with:
  - Increased difficulty factor.
  - New background buildings.
  - New platform and ladder layout.
  - Newly generated enemies and one merchant.
- Player position is reset near the left side for the next Area.

FUTURE / OPEN MECHANICS
-----------------------
- Complete procedural ranged weapon generation in merchants/enemies using the
  type-biased multipliers.
- Implement ranged enemy types (bows, wands, staffs).
- Improve enemy AI with patrol paths, line-of-sight logic, and better
  platform navigation.
- More item types (spears, greatswords, crossbows, magical foci).
- Distinctive visuals per rarity tier (e.g., colored outlines or glows).
- Visual coin drops with pickup animations and sounds.
- More nuanced armor slots and separate stats for helmet, chest, and leggings.
